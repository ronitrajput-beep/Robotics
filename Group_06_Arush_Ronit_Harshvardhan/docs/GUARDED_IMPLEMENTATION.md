# Guarded development prototype

First physical pilot: fixed 0.8 m brush, explicit transparent collision guards and a wider nine-row route. Seed 0 completed all 18 waypoints and settled DOCKED in 344.264 s. New-grid qualifying projected footprint: 90.292% of the entire 24 m². This is a contact-gated footprint proxy, not demonstrated removal of visible dirt. Guard normal impulse was 1337.43 N s, peak normal force 572.81 N: the robot actively relies on containment. Minimum oriented chassis-box distances to left/right/lower/upper glass boundaries were 0.0627/0.1203/0.0479/0.1246 m. It approaches the edges more closely but has not touched all four with its body.

Source hashes and raw 500 Hz telemetry are in analytics/guarded_pilot0.*. Base model and standard historical routes remain separate. Raised trim is visual in the guarded model; containment is supplied by taller transparent collision walls, which collide with chassis/rollers. Brush does not collide with the containment wall, modelling a flexible overhanging cleaning tool; this is an explicit approximation. No robot state is clamped or reset during motion.

Initial RGB prototype locates rendered brown dirt from camera images and calibrated plane projection. Initial tests also exposed false detections on the orange chassis and view-dependent colour thresholds. Those artifacts are retained; perception is not yet ready to trigger autonomous cleaning. The first checkpoint does not implement a full event-driven mission or physical crumb removal.

## Vision/deposition integration progress

The next model uses cyan chassis visuals to avoid confusing brown dirt with the orange body, a fixed 0.8 m brush, a wider chassis box and compiled-active fine-particle collision geometry. Unspawned particles are parked transparently above the scene with gravity compensation; spawning explicitly initialises debris state only. On real panel contact, fine particles convert into a visible surface-layer proxy. That conversion discards their velocity and is logged as a hybrid modelling assumption, not microscopic adhesion physics. Robot state is never reset during the run.

`docs/validation/camera_pipeline_v1` verifies actual RGB renders: no detections in the controlled clean scene, three of three deposited patches detected after multi-camera fusion, panel-coordinate errors 8.4–10.7 mm. This is a controlled-colour detector and calibrated plane projection, not a trained general-purpose detector or vision-based robot localisation.

`analytics/vision_clean_pilot2` and `vision_subthreshold_pilot2` independently complete 12 s at home without triggering. The latter has one genuinely deposited patch which remains uncleared. `docs/validation/idle_trigger_checks.json` checks duration, no sweep, no waypoint progression and actual deposition. Earlier failed/invalid pilots are retained with corrections in `analytics/vision_early_pilot_corrections.json`; they must not count as valid idle/cleaning trials.

Physical larger-crumb ejection is demonstrated separately in `analytics/physical_crumb_pilot0`: actual reverse-rotating brush contact moves a rigid crumb completely beyond the glass edge into an explicit catch tray. It remains a rigid body throughout. This is one near-edge ejection experiment, not yet integrated arbitrary central-crumb collection. The 29-test suite includes the real physical interaction.

An integrated multi-patch scenario is under development: camera-only persistent detection gates departure, the robot sweeps with explicit physical containment, locally qualified brush contact reduces visible fine dirt, and at most two camera-directed revisit cycles can be scheduled before return-home completion. Ground-truth robot navigation remains. Report residual targets and hidden scoring separately rather than equating camera silence with successful cleaning. No final batch or polished new video is claimed at this checkpoint.
